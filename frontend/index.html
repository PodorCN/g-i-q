        Methodology

        Axes. x = g − g* and y = π − π*, where g is expected real GDP growth over the next 6–12 months, g* is trend growth (%), π is expected core PCE inflation and π* is the Fed's target (%). Units are percentage points.

        House views. Each house's latest commentary is read and scored with a fixed rubric: explicit forecasts are converted directly (CPI forecasts minus 0.4pp to put them on a PCE basis); qualitative views map to anchors (e.g. "sticky above target" = +1.0pp). The 1-sigma σ is 0.3pp for point forecasts, 0.5pp for ranges, 0.8pp for qualitative views, +0.2pp when the house stresses tail risks. Views older than days are marked stale (hollow, dashed) and down-weighted.

        Hard-data model. Each FRED indicator is converted into an implied gap with a documented rule of thumb (Okun's law for unemployment, CFNAI × 1.5, CPI − 0.4pp for PCE, etc.). The model point is the weighted mean of those gaps; its σ is their weighted dispersion plus a measurement floor. The history trail re-runs the model month by month on current-vintage data with approximate publication lags (not true real-time vintages).

        Confidence regions. Ellipse semi-axes are k·σx and k·σy with k = √χ²₂(p) (1.52 for 68%, 2.15 for 90%), treating the two axes as independent.

        Quadrant probabilities. Every view is a normal distribution N(μ, σ); the houses form a weighted mixture (weights = confidence × staleness). "Blended" mixes the houses with the hard-data model, which gets of the weight. Probabilities are Monte Carlo shares of draws landing in each quadrant.

        · Data: FRED (St. Louis Fed) and public commentary from each institution. Not investment advice.
