        Methodology, parameters & data sources

        Axes. x = g − g* and y = π − π*, where g is expected real GDP growth over the next 6–12 months, g* is trend growth (%), π is expected core PCE inflation and π* is the Fed's target (%). Units are percentage points (pp).

        House views. Each house's latest commentary is read and scored with a fixed rubric: explicit forecasts convert directly (CPI forecasts minus 0.4pp to convert to PCE); qualitative views map to anchors. The 1-sigma σ is 0.3pp for point forecasts, 0.5pp for ranges, 0.8pp for qualitative views, +0.2pp when tail risks are emphasized. Views older than days are marked stale and down-weighted.

        Quadrant-constrained confidence clouds. A house's uncertainty cloud only crosses into another quadrant if its commentary explicitly mentions or implies a scenario/risk in that quadrant. Each house follows a quadrant-truncated bivariate normal calibrated so 68% or 90% of its truncated probability mass lies inside the cloud.

        Hard-data model. Each FRED indicator is mapped to an implied gap with a documented rule of thumb (Okun's law, CFNAI × 1.5, CPI − 0.4pp, etc.). The model point is the weighted mean across the 16 indicators and is plotted as an observed benchmark point (without a forecast circle).

        Quadrant probabilities. House mixture weights equal confidence × staleness; probabilities are Monte Carlo draws from the quadrant-truncated house views (or, under the Hard data tab, from the indicator dispersion around the model point).

        Sources & disclaimer. · Data: FRED (St. Louis Fed) and public commentary from each institution. Not investment advice.
